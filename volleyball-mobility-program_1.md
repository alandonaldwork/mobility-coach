# Elite Volleyball Daily Mobility & Stretching Program

**Goal:** Maximize active, usable range of motion in ankles, hips, thoracic spine, and shoulders — without borrowing from jump/hit/strength performance — while directly counteracting hours of desk sitting.
**Time budget:** 25–30 min/day (5 min desk reset + 20–25 min main session), plus optional repeatable desk breaks.

---

## 0. Design Review (done before finalizing this program)

Checked against the questions that matter most for an elite athlete:

- **Appropriate for elite level?** Yes — built around volleyball-specific joint demands (approach, landing, hitting arc, defensive stance), not a generic stretch list.
- **Too much volume?** No — daily core work is capped at ~25 min; nothing here should leave you fatigued or sore for training.
- **Interferes with explosiveness?** Avoided by design: static/passive stretching is restricted to *after* training, on rest days, or before bed. Pre-training and pre-jump work is active/dynamic only (see Section 5).
- **Enough active control vs. passive flexibility?** ~70% of the library is active mobility, activation, or control work; passive stretching is the minority and time-gated.
- **Sitting effects addressed?** Yes — a dedicated, repeatable 5-minute desk reset plus daily hip flexor, thoracic extension, and deep neck flexor work.
- **All four priority regions covered?** Yes — ankle and hip appear 4x/week each, thoracic spine 4x/week, shoulder 4x/week, with neck/wrist folded into the daily desk reset.
- **Genuinely under 30 min?** Yes, every day totals 23–27 min.
- **Sustainable daily?** Rotation prevents monotony; a "minimum effective dose" version exists for slammed days (Section 11).
- **Redundant exercises trimmed?** The library is intentionally kept to ~32 exercises total, each targeting a distinct joint action — nothing is included "just because."

This is the trimmed, reviewed version — not a first draft.

## 4. Exercise Library (full technique reference — read once, use daily)

Organized by region. Each entry: **Target · Why it matters · Type · Default dose · Intensity · Breathing · Cues · Common mistakes · Best time.**

### Ankle

**1. Ankle CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations)**
Target: full ankle capsule/proprioception · Why: maintains joint health under volleyball's high jump-landing frequency and supports lateral movement stability · Type: Active mobility/control · Dose: 5 slow circles/direction/side · Intensity: light, full range · Cues: isolate the ankle, keep the knee still · Mistake: moving from the knee/hip instead of the ankle · Best time: anytime — great warm-up or desk-reset filler.

**2. Knee-to-Wall Dorsiflexion Mobilization**
Target: ankle dorsiflexion (tibia-over-foot glide) · Why: closed-chain dorsiflexion range reduces compensations (heel lift, knee valgus) during landing, squatting, and defensive positions · Type: Active mobility · Dose: 8 reps/side x 2 sets, 2-1-2 tempo · Intensity: controlled, moderate · Cues: heel glued down, knee tracks over 2nd/3rd toe · Mistake: heel lifting, knee caving in · Best time: warm-up, main session.

**3. Half-Kneeling Banded Ankle Distraction** *(if band available)*
Target: posterior ankle glide · Why: opens dorsiflexion range without the heel-lift compensation the knee-to-wall drill can mask · Type: Active mobility, band-assisted · Dose: 6–8 reps/side, 2 sec hold at end range · Intensity: light band tension · Cues: band pulls the ankle posteriorly; drive the knee forward · Mistake: too much tension forcing range · Best time: main session, post-training.

**4. Soleus Wall Calf Stretch (bent knee)**
Target: soleus · Why: soleus controls eccentric landing absorption over hundreds of jumps per week; tightness limits ankle rocker in the approach · Type: Passive stretch · Dose: 30–40 sec/side x 2 · Intensity: mild-moderate pull · Cues: heel stays down, bend the back knee until you feel it low in the calf · Mistake: bouncing · Best time: **post-training, desk reset, or before bed only — not pre-explosive work.**

**5. Gastrocnemius Wall Calf Stretch (straight knee)**
Same as above with a straight back knee (targets gastroc). Same dosing and timing restriction.

### Hip

**6. 90/90 Hip Switches**
Target: hip internal/external rotation · Why: IR/ER drives approach footwork, block/dig transitions, and reduces compensatory lumbar rotation · Type: Active mobility · Dose: 6–8 controlled switches/side · Cues: chest tall, control the transition, no momentum · Mistake: using momentum or lifting the hips to cheat range · Best time: warm-up, main session.

**7. World's Greatest Stretch**
Target: hip flexor + hamstring + t-spine combo · Why: mirrors the multi-planar demand of the approach and arm-swing in one flowing movement · Type: Dynamic mobility · Dose: 5 reps/side · Cues: back leg stays straight and glute-engaged, rotate from the mid-back · Mistake: rounding the lower back · Best time: pre-training warm-up.

**8. Couch Stretch**
Target: hip flexor/quad · Why: sitting shortens hip flexors, which limits the hip extension needed for full arm-swing and can load the low back · Type: Passive stretch · Dose: 30–45 sec/side x 2 · Cues: posterior pelvic tilt (squeeze the glute), torso upright · Mistake: arching the low back to fake range · Best time: **post-training, recovery day, before bed — not pre-explosive work.**

**9. Cossack Squat**
Target: adductors, hip · Why: adductor length under active control mirrors lateral defensive loading; groin strains are a common volleyball injury from exactly this pattern · Type: Active mobility/control · Dose: 6–8 reps/side, bodyweight · Cues: heels down as long as possible, chest up, sit into the hip · Mistake: knee caving in, heel popping up early · Best time: warm-up, main session.

**10. Deep Squat Rock**
Target: combined ankle/hip flexion · Why: reflects the low defensive/passing stance · Type: Active mobility · Dose: 30–45 sec hold, gently rocking/shifting weight · Cues: use a doorframe for support if needed, chest tall · Mistake: heels lifting, rounding the spine to force depth · Best time: warm-up, main, desk reset.

**11. Standing Figure-4 / Glute-Piriformis Stretch**
Target: glute/piriformis · Why: tightness here affects the hip IR needed for cutting and pivoting · Type: Passive stretch · Dose: 30 sec/side x 2 · Best time: post-training, recovery day.

**12. Active Straight Leg Raise (Hamstring Floss)**
Target: hamstrings, under active control · Why: active hamstring range supports approach stride length with less strain risk than static-only flexibility work · Type: Active mobility/control · Dose: 8–10 reps/side · Cues: opposite leg stays flat on the floor, dorsiflex the raised foot · Mistake: yanking with the hands, letting the hips rotate · Best time: warm-up, main session.

### Thoracic Spine

**13. Open Book Thoracic Rotation**
Target: t-spine rotation · Why: rotation drives approach/arm-swing sequencing and directly counters flexed sitting posture · Type: Active/dynamic mobility · Dose: 6–8 reps/side · Cues: hips stay stacked, rotate from the mid-back not the lumbar spine · Mistake: rotating from the low back or hips · Best time: desk reset, warm-up, main session.

**14. Quadruped Thread-the-Needle**
Same target/rationale as above, kneeling. Dose: 6/side. Best time: main session, recovery day.

**15. Thoracic Extension Over Foam Roller**
Target: t-spine extension · Why: directly counters kyphotic desk posture; extension range supports the overhead contact point on serves and attacks · Type: Active/passive mobility · Dose: 8–10 slow extensions, or 30–45 sec hold with gentle movement · Cues: support the head with your hands, hinge at the roller contact point, keep ribs down · Mistake: extending from the low back instead of mid-back · Best time: desk reset, post-training, before bed.

**16. Cat-Cow Flow**
Target: general spinal segment mobility · Why: gentle warm-up, ties breath to movement · Type: Active mobility · Dose: 8–10 slow reps · Best time: any, especially desk reset/warm-up.

### Shoulder

**17. Scapular Wall Slides**
Target: scapular upward rotation/posterior tilt · Why: needed for safe overhead loading during attack/serve; directly counters rounded desk posture · Type: Activation/stability · Dose: 10–12 reps, slow · Cues: low back stays flat on the wall, elbows/wrists stay on the wall · Mistake: shrugging, arching the low back to fake range · Best time: desk reset, warm-up, main session.

**18. Band Pull-Aparts**
Target: posterior shoulder/scapular retractors · Why: balances the anterior-dominant loading of high-volume hitting · Type: Activation · Dose: 15–20 reps, light-moderate band · Best time: warm-up, desk reset.

**19. Band External Rotation at 90/90**
Target: rotator cuff, cocking-phase position · Why: trains the exact joint position and range used in the cocking phase of the arm swing — a staple in overhead-athlete shoulder-care protocols · Type: Activation/control · Dose: 10–12 reps/side · Cues: elbow pinned at shoulder height, no shrugging or elbow drift · Mistake: using momentum · Best time: warm-up (light load), post-training.

**20. Sleeper Stretch (posterior capsule)**
Target: posterior shoulder capsule · Why: repeated hitting can tighten this area (posterior shoulder tightness is common in overhead athletes); gentle maintenance can help, but this one needs restraint · Type: Passive stretch, gentle only · Dose: 20–30 sec x 2 · **Caution: stop at any pinching or sharp pain; if you have current shoulder pain, get it assessed before doing this.** · Best time: post-training, recovery days — skip if any shoulder pain is present.

**21. Cross-Body Shoulder Stretch**
Target: posterior/lateral shoulder · Dose: 20–30 sec/side · Type: Passive · Best time: desk reset, post-training.

**22. Shoulder CARs**
Target: full active shoulder range/control · Type: Active mobility/control · Dose: 4–5 slow circles/direction/side · Best time: warm-up, main session.

**23. Wall Overhead Reach (lat/t-spine combo)**
Target: overhead flexion tied to t-spine extension · Why: mirrors the contact point at the top of the swing · Type: Active mobility · Dose: 8 reps, 2 sec hold at top · Best time: warm-up, main session.

### Neck / Upper Back

**24. Chin Tucks**
Target: deep neck flexors · Why: directly counters forward-head posture from screen work; supports head control during play · Type: Activation · Dose: 10 reps, 2 sec hold · Best time: desk reset — repeat often.

**25. Upper Trap Stretch** — Passive, 20–30 sec/side · desk reset.
**26. Levator Scapulae Stretch** — Passive, 20–30 sec/side · desk reset.
**27. Neck Rotation/Lateral Flexion Active ROM** — Active mobility, 5–6 reps each direction · desk reset, warm-up.

### Wrist / Forearm

**28. Wrist Flexor/Extensor Stretch**
Target: wrist flexors/extensors · Why: repetitive ball contact and typing both load these — maintaining range supports contact mechanics and reduces overuse strain · Type: Passive · Dose: 20–30 sec each position x 2 · Best time: desk reset, post-training.

**29. Wrist CARs / Circles** — Active mobility, 5–6 circles each direction · desk reset, warm-up.
**30. Prayer Stretch** — Passive, bilateral wrist flexors, 20–30 sec · desk reset.

### Full-Body Integration

**31. Lateral Lunge with Reach**
Target: adductors + t-spine rotation · Why: mirrors the lateral defensive shuffle and blocking footwork combined with the reach/rotation needed to play a ball off-center · Type: Dynamic mobility · Dose: 6 reps/side · Cues: sit back into the bent-leg hip, straight leg's foot stays flat, reach the opposite arm across · Mistake: knee traveling too far past the toes, rounding the back · Best time: warm-up, main session.

**32. Inchworm-to-Cobra Flow**
Target: posterior chain + shoulder + t-spine, connected · Why: a good "systems check" linking hamstring/calf length to shoulder and t-spine extension in one continuous movement · Type: Dynamic mobility/integration · Dose: 3–4 slow reps · Cues: walk the hands out with soft knees, press through the shoulders at the bottom, keep the hips low in cobra · Mistake: rushing, hips too high in cobra (turns it into downward dog) · Best time: warm-up, main session.

## 5. Part C — Modifying Any Day Based on Today's Training

**The core rule:** on days with explosive work (training, match, jump session), keep pre-activity work **active/dynamic/activation only** — skip anything tagged *Passive stretch* in the library. Save passive holds for after the activity, a recovery day, or before bed. This avoids the acute, transient strength/power dip that heavy static stretching can cause right before explosive output.

| Context | Timing | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Volleyball training (skills/tactical) | Before | Active/dynamic items only from that day's list, trimmed to ~10–12 min |
| | After | Full session as programmed; passive holds are fine, can run 5–10 sec longer |
| Match day | Before | Trimmed active-only circuit, ~6–8 min: ankle + hip + shoulder CARs, deep squat rock, 90/90 switches, band pull-aparts/ER |
| | After | Full session if time allows; otherwise defer passive work (especially calf and hip flexor) to the next recovery day |
| Heavy strength — lower body | Before | Active ankle/hip only; skip deep passive hip flexor/hamstring holds pre-lift |
| | After | Full session; a good day for couch stretch and hamstring floss |
| Heavy strength — upper body | Before | Activation only (band pull-aparts, wall slides); skip sleeper/cross-body stretch |
| | After | Full shoulder session is fine |
| Jump training / plyometrics | Before | Ankle/calf **active** work only (CARs, dorsiflexion drill, deep squat rock); skip static calf stretching — calf/Achilles stiffness contributes to reactive strength | 
| | After | Full session, including calf stretches |
| Recovery/rest day | Anytime | Full session, optionally extended with foam rolling and longer passive holds; the best day to run your self-assessment tests |

**Worked example — Day 1 in "Pre-Training Mode":** run #1, #2, #10, #6, #9, #12, #7 exactly as programmed (all active/dynamic), and simply drop the closing wall calf stretch (#4/#5), replacing it with one more set of ankle CARs. Session shrinks to ~19 min instead of 22. The same drop-the-passive-items logic applies to whichever day you're on.

## 7. 4–6 Week Progression

**Weeks 1–2 — Foundation.** Priority is consistency and clean movement, not range. Run every prescribed dose exactly as written; don't chase depth. Nail the breathing and cues before adding anything.

**Weeks 3–4 — Progression.** Once an exercise feels "too easy" (see criteria below), progress it one of these ways — pick one change per exercise, not all at once:
- Passive holds: add 5–10 sec per stretch, up to a ~45–60 sec ceiling.
- Active mobility: add 2 reps, or slow the tempo (e.g., 3-1-3 count) for more time under control.
- Add light external load where it doesn't compromise form (e.g., a light band on 90/90, a light plate held at the chest during the deep squat hold).
- Where relevant, shift a pure static hold toward an end-range isometric (e.g., a controlled pause-and-hold at the end of the ASLR rather than a passed stretch) — this is where "flexibility" starts becoming "control," which is the actual target.

**Weeks 5–6 — Integration.** The real test is whether new range shows up in movement, not just in the stretch position — deeper, more controlled landings; a fuller arm-swing at the top; a more comfortable low defensive stance. Keep testing every 1–2 weeks (Section 8). If a region plateaus across two consecutive tests, drop its dose to a maintenance level (half the reps/sets, same frequency) and redirect the time to a region still improving.

**Signs an exercise is ready to progress:**
- You hit the full prescribed rep/time with zero compensation (no heel lift, no spinal rounding, no momentum) and clearly have range to spare.
- A passive hold stops producing a real stretch sensation well before time is up.
- No residual tightness or soreness from the exercise itself afterward.

## 8. Self-Assessment Tests (every 1–2 weeks)

Run these on a recovery day, same time of day, ideally after a light general warm-up. Track qualitative notes and side-to-side symmetry — a phone photo/video for angle checks works better than trying to eyeball degrees. **Symmetry is often the more meaningful marker for injury-risk purposes than absolute range.**

- **Knee-to-Wall Ankle Test:** half-kneeling, heel down, note how far the toes can sit from the wall while the knee still touches it, each side.
- **Deep Squat Assessment:** heels-down depth, torso angle, and whether one side breaks down before the other.
- **90/90 Hip Rotation:** seated 90/90 position, note ease/comfort and range on each side.
- **Hip Extension Check:** how the couch stretch feels/looks each side — note any obvious asymmetry.
- **Shoulder Overhead Flexion (Wall Angel):** arms overhead against a wall, note any gap at the low back or how close the arms get to the wall.
- **Shoulder IR/Sleeper Check:** compare ease side-to-side; flag (and get assessed) if asymmetry is increasing rather than closing.
- **Thoracic Rotation:** seated, arms crossed, rotate and compare degrees side-to-side using a mirror or phone.

Don't expect a straight line — expect fewer compensations, better symmetry, and range that shows up in actual play over weeks, not a specific number.

## 10. Safety & Recovery Guidance

- **Normal vs. not normal:** a mild-to-moderate pulling/tension sensation that eases within the hold is expected. Sharp, localized, radiating, or numbness/tingling sensations are not — stop immediately if you feel any of those.
- **Effort level:** mobility work should sit around 4–6/10 effort. It should never exceed ~7/10.
- **When not to push range:** skip aggressive stretching on any acutely swollen or inflamed joint, anything recently injured without clearance, or any area that's sharp-painful during normal daily movement.
- **Sore calves/knees/hips/groin/low back/shoulders:** ordinary training soreness is diffuse, roughly symmetric, and improves with light movement over 24–72 hours. Something that's sharp, one-sided, swollen, mechanically blocking (a joint that "catches" or won't move through its normal range), or that keeps flaring in the exact same spot is worth having assessed by a sports medicine physician or physical therapist rather than stretched through — this is especially true for the groin (adductor strains can worsen with continued loading) and the knees, given your jump volume.
- This document is general educational guidance, not a substitute for individualized assessment — particularly at your training load, a sports PT baseline screen is worth having if you haven't had one recently.
